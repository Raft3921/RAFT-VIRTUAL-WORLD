# Continuous final shot, prone transitions and synchronized footprints — trails110

The first replay cut frames the shooter for 500 ms, fires with the shot sound, and hands focus to a detailed projectile over 180 ms. The projectile follows the full muzzle-to-impact segment and arrives 120 ms before the existing second cut. Contact effects and the victim fall share that impact timestamp, with no second impact at the cut. The falling victim and overview result cuts and two-second skip-to-overview remain. Replays use the server's captured firing position, orientation and stance rather than moving the shooter to their later impact-time position. Captured firing data is internal until needed by a final-hit replay, so normal projectile broadcasts do not grow. The rail uses smaller clearance for the close projectile camera and retains obstacle-aware path selection and the safe fixed fallback when no continuous route exists. Decorative projectile rings and streaks are hidden in this presentation.

Prone pelvis, torso and limbs blend over several frames instead of switching instantly. Head orientation counters the pelvis and torso rotation to face forward and preserves aim turns. Eye height and first-person hands follow the same descent/ascent rates. Mesh bounds continue to prevent ground penetration. The prone physical/hit envelope is now 0.62 m to accommodate the forward-facing head. Jump and landing dust are larger with more visible puffs, retaining the existing bounded particle pool.

The server derives alternating footprints from accepted movement distance and grounded activity state. Airborne, flight, prone, seated, sleeping, ragdoll, mantle, eliminated, suspended and teleport segments cannot produce walking footprints. Foot dimensions match the 4x4-pixel soles of the standard and supplied JEM models, with shorter JEM stride. Marks use authoritative support heights and ramp normals, are broadcast to connected players, and are included in the world snapshot for late joiners. The client renders square sole impressions in one instanced batch; a shader fades each mark continuously over 60 real-time seconds. Marks are capped at 4096 on server and client, visually culled beyond 100 m, and gun-zone marks are removed when the round map resets. Tracks are transient room state and are not written to storage.

No tests, runtime checks or browser verification were run, following the user's prior instruction. Actual multiplayer appearance and camera transitions remain unverified. Deployment outcomes are reported separately through publication metadata.
